T 417 The Copper Ring

   You spread your fingers. The ring looks like nothing at all: a circle of
copper with no engraving, given to you by an old woman you had helped up on
the King's Road. It seemed too large for you; an hour later it fitted
perfectly.

   You know what it is worth now. As long as it is on your finger you always
know where north lies and you never lose your way, neither in the darkest
forest nor in the most twisted streets. That is a great deal already for a man
who means to enter a swamp no one has ever managed to map.

   It does more. It grows warm whenever you are in the presence of an evil
being, even one who makes a great show of friendship. It stayed cold for a
whole week among brigands, rough and brutal men yet honest in their own
fashion; it warned you, on the other hand, outside caves where black magic was
practised.

   Warm before Evil, cold before Good: there is your compass.
